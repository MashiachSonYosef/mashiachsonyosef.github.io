## What that means, and where I stop

The two cases share a glyph and nothing else:

- **Joshua 21:36–37** — the pair is missing from the base. Presence
  divergence between editions. Attested, famous, settled.
- **Isaiah 50:10–11** — the pair is present in both, counts identical. The
  brackets say something ABOUT the text rather than whether it is there.

Candidates I can name but not attest: an alternate targumic rendering
(*targum acher*), or supplementary expansive material of the
tosefta-targum kind, which printed editions of Targum Jonathan to the
Prophets do commonly set off. I am not going to pick one. That would be a
guess wearing a finding's clothes, and the frame's own rule applies — the
kind of a mark is attested, never inferred from the glyph.

**The frame-correct handling:** these are Q sites — marks the source made in
the text — of UNDETERMINED KIND. Record them as that, with the glyph, the
span, and the test result showing presence-vs-parallel, and let a witness
settle the kind. Your bracket detector is the right tool and should keep
running; it just should not name what it finds.

**And the structural lesson worth keeping:** the same notation means
different things in different places. That is exactly why Q types marks by
kind, and exactly why kind cannot be read off the glyph. Your detector found
two spans with one shape and two meanings, which is the strongest possible
argument for the rule.
