## What this means for the page lane

The page lane owns its end. Its decisions stand on the sources' own files and on the lane's own reasons, written down where the decision is made. They do not stand on the owner's words.

The owner's words are a handful of basics about what the reader is:
- a word has a line;
- a card has its reading pills and the frame around them (divisions and compspan, the selectable gloss, a pressed pill setting the line).

Beyond these, nothing is put to the owner to rule. A note or a ship report never gives «the owner said» as the reason for a choice about readings, sources or the page. It gives the evidence: what the source's own file says, what was measured, and why this choice and not the other.

The lane's one rule is the lane's own, made from what the sources showed: a reading stands at a word only where a source puts it there by its own address, or by a bridge a source declares. Nothing is matched by bare letters. It stands because the evidence holds it up. If the evidence turns against it, the lane changes it.

## What still goes to the owner

Only what the lane cannot do itself:
- speaking in the owner's name to someone outside the project, such as a note to STEP;
- what only the owner can reach: accounts, dashboards, bucket settings, payments;
- a download or host the environment refuses. Name it, and do not route around it.

## The lane's design principle: room for everything, the source's own cut by default

The reader is built to hold every cut a source makes: a word's divisions, a run's card across words, each word a press away inside it. That is its capacity, and the capacity is as large as it can be. It is not the default.

At rest the page shows the cut the place's own source makes, its own unit, whether that is finer or coarser than our text's. Another source's cut stands inside the same card as an option. Capacity is what lets the default stay simple and true. Forcing the finest cut everywhere is the mistake to avoid.
