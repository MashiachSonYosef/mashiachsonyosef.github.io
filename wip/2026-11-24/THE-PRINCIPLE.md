# Serve each source as it serves itself

The owner, 24 November 2026: «serve step as step serves, done. serve the next dictionary as it is suggested to serve, etc. and the overlap is the struggle»; «in theory if another dictionary made a different cut our system should be able to hold both»; «cut back to exactly the part of the project that will stand forever, and then we can consider adding to it the other dictionarys back in 1 by 1».

- Each source keeps its own division of a word, held as it gives it, never folded into ours.
- Each source is read only against its own division: its readings sit on its own pieces.
- On the thirty-nine books the line at rest follows STEP: its division, its tag for each piece, its reading. A dictionary row shows only where its entry is the one STEP's tag names (which also keeps a homonym off a word it is not). Where our text (Sefaria's Miqra according to the Masorah) spells a word otherwise than STEP's (the Leningrad Codex), the spelling bridge maps one to the other; that is the one thing of our own this needs.
- Another source switched on shows its readings on its own division, beside STEP's.
- The overlap, where two divisions disagree under the same letters, is shown honestly, listed, and ruled; never settled silently by a rule of ours.
- Every place our page departs from STEP is listed with its count, the first job of the review week, and each rule behind a departure is sorted in one of two ways. It **reconciles** sources' own answers (holds two divisions side by side, or decides which leads at rest by the owner's ruling): that is the machinery the project needs, and it stays. Or it **overrides** a source's own answer (we cut what STEP leaves whole, or grade away a reading STEP gives): that is the fault, and the place goes back to the source's answer. Candidates to sort: the joining dagesh, the grading of one-letter pieces as prefix or suffix, the pointing grade, spellings matched with no STEP tag behind them.
- The foundation is the thirty-nine books with the twelve Bible dictionaries, made right first; the other dictionaries come back one at a time after, and other works (the Tikkunei Zohar) after that.

## The lesson (the owner, 24 November 2026: «keep in mind this difficulty was found within work that was essentially already done for us»)

STEP gave us the thirty-nine books divided and tagged, and most of our trouble came from what we added on top. So: before any rule of our own, ask whether the source already answers the question; if it does, follow it. A rule of our own needs a stated reason and the owner's ruling, and stands on a list the owner can read. A work with no STEP behind it (the Tikkunei Zohar) means the dividing and tagging are ours, which is why it waits until the foundation is right.

The owner, 24 November 2026, correcting the lesson above: «our own cuts isnt what id define as the problem because we still may need a system that handles 2 competing systems a la dictionarys at once». So the fault is not a rule of ours as such; it is a rule of ours that replaces an answer the source already gave. Machinery that holds and reconciles several sources' own answers is the work itself.
