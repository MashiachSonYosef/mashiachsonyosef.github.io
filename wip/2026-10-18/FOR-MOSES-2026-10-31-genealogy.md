1. **Nineteen rows give a kin phrase to the wrong person.** The source attaches the phrase to a third person named in the same unit, and the row attaches it to the entry's person. Eleven of the nineteen have both ends cited. The cause is in the RULE as amended: §4.6 (ABOUT_THE_OBJECT) tests only a match that follows a previous kin match and exempts a gap that begins "and " or "or "; §4.7 tests possessive matches only. A kin phrase after a name brought in by a verb or a non-kin relation (married, addressed by, killed by, together with, friend of, concubine of, opponent of), or after "and" in a chain describing that name, is about that name. Ask: amend the rule so these are withheld, re-run, and list the nineteen in the relay with what becomes of each.
