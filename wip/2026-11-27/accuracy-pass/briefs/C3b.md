# Helper C3b: these dictionaries, each as it serves itself

**Your sources:** the Open Scriptures Hebrew Lexical Index (M33, `openscriptures_lexical_index`), the unfoldingWord Hebrew-Aramaic Lexicon (M7, `unfoldingword_uhal`), and the BiblicalTools Living Lexicon (M45, `biblicaltools_living_lexicon`). Confirm each M-id and key in `data/route-store/index.json` (`m_sources`) before you start; a source may have more than one M-id.

**Question.** Does every reading we print from each of these say what the source itself says, in the form the source's own marks ask for?

For each source, in this order:

1. **Its bytes.** Find the file as received (`data/source-corpus-v1.json`, `data/dictionary-kinds-v1.json`, the source's folder under `data/ledgers-v1/`, the store under `v2/sources/`; `data/store-path-map-v1.json` for moved addresses). Fetch it and say what it is: format, fields, how an entry is keyed, what it says about itself (title, license, year, version).
2. **Every served row against the source, by machine.** For each row of this source in the route store: find the source entry it came from; apply the declared steps (the ledger's division and print steps in `data/ledgers-v1/<key>/`, the made spaces, the strikes, the sense openings where they apply); is the served text exactly what results? Count exact, differs, source entry not found. List what differs, grouped by the kind of difference.
3. **The source's own marks.** Count every mark in the source's raw readings and in our served rows: arrows, commas, semicolons, slashes both ways, angle brackets, square brackets, round brackets, commas inside brackets, colons, dashes, quotation marks, numbered senses, cross-references («see», «compare», «id.», «q.v.»), abbreviations, and anything else that recurs. For each: what the source's own front matter or documentation says it means (quote it, with where you found it); the rule the ledger declares for it; and whether the printed pill honors that meaning. **A mark with no declared rule is a finding. A declared rule that contradicts the source's own explanation of its mark is a finding.**
4. **How the source divides a word and binds a reading to a place.** What does the source itself give for that (a Strong's number, its own entry id, pointing, a verse reference, a part of speech, a stem)? Which of those do we use to decide where its reading shows, and which do we hold but ignore? Each ignored one is a candidate «applied badly».
5. **On the page.** Open 60 cards across at least 10 books where this source shows a pill (stratify by the mark classes of step 3 and include its longest and oddest rows). For each: the pill as printed, the source entry, and your judgment: says what the source says / changed the sense / cut wrongly / belongs to another entry or another word / unreadable. Also read its credit, license and year on the card against what the source says of itself.

Output: one `findings.md` with a section per source, plus per source `rows-differ-<key>.tsv.gz`, `marks-<key>.tsv`, `cards-<key>.tsv`.
