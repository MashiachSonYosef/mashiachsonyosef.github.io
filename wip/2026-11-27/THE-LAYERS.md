## What the eight reports say when read together

Two systems live on the page.

1. **Each source answering at the place, by its own address.** The line under the word (STEP's own words for this word of this verse) and the card's lines «here STEP / MACULA / UBS / BDB reads». The reports found this nearly clean: 5 lines wrong in Ruth's 1,296, none in Genesis 1 to 5; STEP's pieces byte for byte at 304,463 of 304,484 words.
2. **The pool of pills, matched by spelling.** Every row of every source whose headword has the letters of whatever block the card is on, then filtered by gates added one at a time (pointing, stem, entry, names, piece class, unpointed rows). Every card fault in the eight reports is here: «beacon» leading «and there was»; «mosquito» on «so»; «whether, direct, indirect» on the article; «mother» under a word that is «if»; BDB's «preposition» as a reading; a reading of STEP's credited to BDB 1906.

On a phone the first is folded away and the second fills the card.

## The root

One join is ours alone: **a row belongs wherever its letters stand.** We made it so that one store could hold every source under one key and be ready for any text and any cut. It throws away each source's own address for its words (this verse and word; this entry; this sense; this form), and every gate since is a piece of that address handed back as a filter. The template of slots (the whole word, each piece, the hyphenated pair, a run of words, each half of a written-and-read pair) was then filled through that join, slot by slot, whether or not any source had put anything there. That is how a definition no dictionary recommended comes to stand under a word.

## The layers

| Layer | Whose | What it does for us | It stands when |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. The text: letters, points, marks, spaces, hyphens, written-and-read pairs | the edition's | it is the thing read | always; held whole |
| 2. Each source's own address for its words: STEP and MACULA by verse, word and piece; Strong's, both BDBs, TBESH, the UBS dictionary and the SDBH by entry, some by sense and by the verses a sense cites; Davidson and Strong's form lists by the spelled form | each source's | says where a source itself puts a reading | always; held whole beside our own cuts, never flattened |
| 3. Bridges between sources' addresses: STEP's tag is a Strong's number by STEP's own design; MACULA marks a word with an SDBH sense; our text against STEP's text where they differ; STEP's letter against BDB's entry (not yet held) | a source's where it declares the bridge; ours where we build it | lets twelve sources answer at one place | when a source declares it, or when ours is a stated rule with a count of where it is exact and where inferred |
| 4. Presentation: a list cut into pills; a word's pieces in frames; two words drawn in one cell; signs hidden; who leads; one pill for the same words from several sources | ours | makes it readable and pressable | as a switch or a frame a reader can see and undo; it never changes what a source is said to say, or whose words they are |
| 5. Matching by spelling | ours alone | readiness for a text no source has tagged; a last resort | only as a named fallback where layers 2 and 3 give nothing; never as the main binding on the 39 books |

A slot of the template is room to hold something. It shows only where a source fills it through layers 2 and 3.

So every change we make to a source's data is one of four kinds: **holding** it (always stands), **converging** it with another source (stands with its reason), **presenting** it (stands as a switch or a frame), or **filling** a slot by spelling where no source spoke (does not stand on the 39 books). An inventory of every change we make, by kind, with what it does and why it stands, is also the note of changes the licenses ask of anyone who reformats their data.
