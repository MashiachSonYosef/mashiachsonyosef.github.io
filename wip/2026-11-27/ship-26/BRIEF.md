## The law to build

**A row prints as a reading only where its source's own structure says it is one; what the source marks as something else prints as that, or not as a pill.** By rule, from the sources' bytes, never by a hand list of readings:

1. **The unabridged BDB.** (a) An italic run whose whole text is one of the provider's written-out grammar words, or an outline letter, is no pill. (b) An italic run inside the provider's transliteration tag, or that the record itself introduces as Latin, German or French, is no pill. (c) An italic run set directly before a reference, after a Hebrew phrase, is the wording of that verse: a reading at that verse, and elsewhere an example on the card (a note), not a pill. Say what the record's own structure gives you to tell these apart, and what it does not; where it does not, leave the row as it is and count it.
2. **BDB, the Open Scriptures edition.** (a) A def written against a letter or a bracket of the entry's running text prints with the word it is part of, read from the entry's own bytes («(sun)set», «I (we) pray»). (b) A def of one letter that is not so completed is no pill. (c) A def in the opening bracket of an entry the file marks `type="root"` is a note of the root, not a pill. (d) A def that a `<ref>` follows, after a Hebrew phrase, is that verse's wording, as in 1(c).
3. **Strong's, the Wikisource edition.** The list of renderings opens at the edition's sign for «:—», whatever the edition wrote there; nothing before it is a pill; a note after the list's stop is the entry's note, printed as a note; a text whose brackets do not balance is held whole («be(-come, accomplished, committed, like)» is one pill), as the Open Scriptures ledger already holds such texts; the list dash is not printed on a rendering.
4. **Strong's, the Open Scriptures edition.** The pointer after the list's stop prints apart from the last rendering, as the file's own link; the rendering before it then joins the same rendering of other sources.

**How it is laid.** The house already has the ways a row prints something other than a pill: a ledger's branch and print step (`data/ledgers-v1/<source key>/`, `tools/apply-ledger-divisions-v1.mjs`, `tools/ledger-print-from-declaration-v1.mjs`: a row may print a part of itself, be held whole, or give no pill); a sense opening that prints as a note (`data/sense-openings/`, `tools/apply-sense-openings-v1.mjs`); a row's facts. Use those. Lay each rule as a pinned list made by a tool of yours from the source's bytes (the source file's sha256, the rule in words, the count), applied by the house's own tool for that way, with a check that holds the count. Prefer a ship that changes no line of `zone.html`: other builders are changing it now. If one of your laws cannot print without a change to the page (an example on the card at another verse), build the data, count it, and say exactly what the page would need; do not edit `zone.html` beyond that without saying which lines.

The ledgers and the print steps came from the corpus lane with the owner's answers on their branches (`data/ledger-branch-answers-v1.json`). Where your rule changes how a branch the owner answered prints, name the branch and the answer, and say why the source's own structure settles it otherwise. Do not delete a row from the store; a row that gives no pill stays a row, its record whole.
