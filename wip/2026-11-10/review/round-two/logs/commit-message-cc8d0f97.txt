The review's second round: who carries a reading is asked of the store in one place, the orders are projected whole, a verse's citations are its own, the chain counts words

The owner, 10 November: "cleaner code is better." The review's first round (the commit before this one) left its third batch open: one rule in one place in the tools. This is that batch, and what the fifth reader's report found that was still unproven. Every fault below was measured on the served files or in a browser before it was changed. The scripts that made each edit, the probes and their logs are on the notes branch (wip/2026-11-10/review/round-two/). Nothing was deleted to make room.

One rule that had four copies. Which rows carry a reading, and which of them is the oldest, was decided again in the gloss line, the lattice projector, the chain builder and the door, and three of the four matched the reading letter for letter where the store groups its pills by the printed form (a closing stop is not another reading). It is one function of the store reader now (gloss-store-v1: carriersOf, wordingYear) and the four call it. What the copies had made wrong:

- Under "masoretic", "vowels differ" and "cites here", the line under a word and the source it credits were taken from part of a reading's carriers. 15,315 of the 180,273 pointed spellings on the 39 books: masoretic 9,188 (1,030 read another reading, 8,138 the same reading credited to a later source, 20 had no line), vowels differ 7,628 (1,061, 6,565, and 2 that now have none), cites here 257. In a browser, on spellings taken from six books (Genesis, Isaiah, Psalms, Ruth, Daniel, Jonah): where the masoretic reading moved, the line and the card's first reading disagreed at 48 of 48 and agree at 48; where its credit moved, the two credits disagreed at 64 of 64 and agree at 64; with the Masorah lifted the credits disagreed at 59 of 59 and agree at 59, and the readings disagreed at 54 of 54 and agree at 43. The 11 left, and 5 words of the credit lists whose readings differ on both builds, are named below.
- The chain books credited a later source than the page for 4,379 of 13,794 first links, counted the scribal marks as words (Amos: "2,221 words, 184 carry none" is 2,042 words, 3 with none, and 179 marks), and printed 24 written-and-read pairs with neither half. The builder counts marks apart and prints a pair's two halves; the index's total is words.
- The door's chip under a book's title named a later source at 15 of the 40 books, and the hover of a reading under a name named a later record at 4 of 55.

The orders the store defines are projected with the table. A zone's line is re-read from the store on every round, and the two order columns beside it (fewest characters, the corpus's own) were written by another tool on another day and not since: the 40 books held 47,038 keys under "characters" where 68,444 are owed and 4,583 under "corpus" where 699 are, and the 21 commentary zones held none (15,578 and 168). On Amos, of 12 words whose key the first column did not hold the line and the card disagreed at 9; all 12 agree. The re-gloss writes both columns whole on every projection, from the same table, in one walk of the zone; the same walk prices the switches, which every commentary zone priced at 0 lines (249,699 lines that change and 97,844 that go bare, summed over their 915 switch sets). Its receipt no longer claims a move it did not make: last_move is written when the table's seal changes and carried forward otherwise, and a second run over its own output writes the same bytes.

"Cites here" is the verse's. The lattice's record of a spelling is taken at its first verse in the book, and every later verse read that verse's list of citing cards: 14,781 of 306,493 graded word places (4,129 of 20,624 in Genesis). The sidecar keeps the first verse's list and, for each other verse whose list differs, that verse's own with its own leader (v[<verse>]: 14,453 verses of 8,212 spellings), and the card's sort and the line both ask for the verse the word stands in. What a reader who chose that order sees: at 1,166 of those verses another reading leads; at 2,314 a card that cites the verse leads where the line was told of none; and at 8,984 no card cites the verse, so the line no longer borrows the reading of a card that cites another verse and reads as the word reads without the order (Amos 1:6: «transgressions of», which a card cites at 1:3, gives way to «rebellion»; Amos 7:8 reads «O Amos», which a card cites there). In a browser on 71 such places in three books: where a card cites the verse the line reads that verse's leader at 21 of 21 (2 before); where none does, it read the first verse's leader at 50 of 50 and reads it at 8, where the word's own reading is the same.

A name is found among the pills by its printed form. Where a name leads a word, the card looked for it letter for letter among pills that keep whichever spelling arrived first, and missed it at 607 of the 2,684 keys a name leads (2,143 words): Amos 1:6's line read «Gaza» and its card opened on «strong».

The hover of the source chip beside a line said "the oldest witness carrying this reading" of every line. As the site opens the line is the place's own reading, credited to the source that reads the word so there, and an older record often carries the same words: 15 of 80 words spread over Amos said it beside a card crediting an older source. That chip says what the card's own place line already says: the source that reads this word so at this place.
