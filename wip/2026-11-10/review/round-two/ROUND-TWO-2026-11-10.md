## 6. Measured and left as it is: the owner's to say

1. **An overlay source on the card.** A reading from one of the overlay sources (Jastrow, the Samaritan lists) is pooled on the card and never carries the line, by the ruling that brought them in. Two things follow, and both are now measured. A card can open on an overlay's reading the line does not read: with the Masorah lifted, 11 of the 54 spellings of R1 (8 of them one word, whose card opens on Jastrow's «strong» where the line reads «ancient time»). And a reading can rank otherwise because an overlay stands behind it: 5 more words in those lists, and Amos 8:3 under "masoretic". In a survey of 80 words spread over Amos the line and the card's first reading agree at 80 as the site opens, at 79 under "masoretic", and at 68 with the Masorah lifted, where 11 of the 12 are these. In every case the reading pressed on the card is the line's. Either the line follows the overlays, or the overlays do not rank a card; it is not mine to pick.
2. **The Masorah lifted where no source points the word otherwise.** The card still lists the sources that are silent on pointing ahead of the ones that agree, and the line keeps its reading: the twelfth word of the 12 (Amos 2:11, whose card opens on an encyclopedia's title).
3. **"Cites here" at a verse no card cites.** At 8,984 verses of the 14,453 the lattice names no card for the verse, and the line used to borrow the reading of a card that cites another verse. It now reads as the word reads without the order, which is oldest first over the whole pool (Amos 1:6: «transgressions of», cited at 1:3, gives way to «rebellion»). That is what the order's own rule says. If such a verse should read the place's own reading instead, as the site does when it opens, that is one rule and the owner's.
4. **The Pages deployment.** The published tree is about 370 MB. GitHub's built-in deployment cancels itself when its file sync takes more than ten minutes. Of the last 18 ships, 6 needed more than one attempt and 2 never deployed until the next ship replaced them (`tools/probes/pages-runs.py` over the Actions record). The first round of this review was pushed at 18:34 UTC and went live at 20:30 on the fifth attempt; the three late successes I watched today each came after 45 minutes or more with no attempt, and then took under a minute. What the failures have in common is not the size of the ship: the first round changed 71 MB of zone files and failed four times, and the second changed 56 MB an hour and a half later and deployed in 69 seconds. They come in spells on GitHub's side, and a spell has outlasted three and four attempts. A deployment workflow of the repository's own could wait longer than ten minutes, which would ride out a slow sync but not a stalled one. It needs the Pages source switched to "GitHub Actions" in the repository's settings, which only the owner can do; I have not verified the longer limit against GitHub's documentation from here and have built nothing.
