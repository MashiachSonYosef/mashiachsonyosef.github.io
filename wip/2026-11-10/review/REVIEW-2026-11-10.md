# The page lane, reviewed (10 November 2026)

The owner, 10 November: "want to do the review now? now that we found so much space from deleting codex we no longer really need to delete old of your stuff so deletion is basically off the list now, but that said we can still switch the backend if it hasnt been. cleaner code is better."

This is the review. It covers what the page lane owns on `main` at efdb4aaa (the build that is live): the page file (`reader/zone.html`), the 291 files of `reader/tools`, the round that is run each time something ships, the checks, the records under `reader/data`, the documents, and the state of the switch to the store's v2 layout. Nothing was deleted. What was fixed is listed at the end with its commit; everything else is listed with where it stands.

## How it was done

- The whole check suite, once, on the live build: 167 checks, 75 minutes, one at a time (`suite-2026-11-10-main-efdb4aaa.log`, beside this file).
- A linter over the page script and every tool (undefined names, unused names, unreachable code; no style rules), and counting scripts for what the page declares and never uses, what the tools repeat, and what the records are read by (`tools/` beside this file).
- Five readers, each given one part and told to change nothing: the page script in thirds (lines 2566 to 7100, 7100 to 11700, 11700 to 16381), the 42 tools nothing calls, and the fourteen tools of the round. Their reports are kept whole in `readers/`. A reader's finding is a claim until it is checked; each finding below says whether I checked it and how.

## The short version

1. **Readers meet faults today that no check sees.** Eleven are proven (a browser, an exported file, or the served files themselves), and about thirty more are reported by the readers with line numbers and not yet proven by me. They are listed in section 1.
2. **Half the checks have not been run since they were written.** The round runs 82 of 167. Run whole, 132 pass, 17 are red and 18 cannot run on this machine. Of the 17: four are red because the runner hands them the wrong argument, five because the records describe a shelf of 3,481 works that the tree has not held since September, seven because a ruled change made the check's own claim stale and nobody ran it, and one is real housekeeping (six tools that write a record and do not say which ledger letter it is for).
3. **Two of those reds I had been reporting to the owner as "the environment's, as always".** They are not. The store record says the shelf stands beside the door and pins 3,595 files; the tree holds 147 of them. The same gap is why my round carries three hand workarounds, and one of those workarounds is why the old address /1kings/ has told readers for weeks that the work behind it is not being served.
4. **The round is not in the repository.** `build.sh` and `PIPELINE.md` describe one pass in which no zone is edited after it is written. What is run is a set of scratch scripts that re-project every zone in place. The copy kept on the notes branch calls three scripts that were never kept. A failed stage does not stop it.
5. **Where a rule is typed twice, the copies have drifted.** Which rows carry a reading, which source is the oldest, how a zone's words are walked, how a rewritten zone is signed, how an open card is opened again: each exists in three to ten copies, and the changes of 6 to 10 November reached some copies and not others. Most of the proven faults are this.
6. **Dead code is not the problem.** The page script declares 1,676 names and seven are unused. The tools carry 66 unused imports and names in 49 files and one builder with about 120 dead lines. What makes the page hard to work on is its shape: one closure of 13,816 lines, 659 statements sharing their state, and one function of 1,974 lines that builds the card.
7. **The switch to the v2 layout is ready on this side.** Every input of the round stands in `v2/` (compspan v13.8 arrived on 8 November), the round was proven from v2 byte for byte on 8 November, and the tools still name the old folders in 15 places. Road A's store has not arrived.

## 3. The shelf is not where the records say

`data/zone-store-v1.json` pins 3,595 files and says `base: null`, which the record defines as "beside the door". The tree holds 147 of them: the 40 served works and their sidecars. The other 3,448 (3,441 works and 7 sidecars) have not been in a tree's tip since September. Their bytes stand in this repository's history on the branch `archive-2026-10-gh-pages` (two sampled: the commits of 2 to 4 September). No served work's file is among them. The serve gate's own rule keeps an unserved work's file out of the published tree, so the store record's older law ("no pin names a file that is not there while the shelf stands beside the door") contradicts the newer one, and has been red since.

What follows from that one gap:

- Five checks red on every checkout of `main`.
- `check-c0-refusals-v1 --write` rebuilds the gate's receipt from the disk and would drop 3,441 works, so my round merges the receipt by hand in Python.
- The door's builder cannot resolve an old address whose work is not on the disk and stops, so my round switches the step off, and /1kings/ went stale (L6).
- `emit-lane-state-v1` writes `zones_on_disk: 40, withheld: 0` beside a gate receipt that says 3,481 on the shelf.
- Three tools would write wrong records if run here (the readers' list: `project-run-cards-v1` numbers its places across whatever is on the disk; `emit-work-attachment-v1` would write an empty record; `restore-shelf-from-history-v1` promises a restore its own later comment says the pins refuse).

The fix does not move the shelf, which is the owner's ruling and still owed (asked 23 October: where the other works' files serve from). It says what is true: the store record declares which pins are held off the door, how many, under what hash of their names, where their bytes stand and since when; the checks hold the tree to that declaration (a served work's file can never be among the held; a file that goes missing is red); the tools that rebuild a shelf-wide record carry the held works' entries instead of dropping them.

## 4. The round, and the pipeline the documents describe

`build.sh`: "There is no step that edits a zone after it is written… Run it twice on the same inputs and the outputs are byte-identical." Its inputs (the mirror, the bridge, the serve files) are not on any machine this lane runs on, and it built none of the 40 zones as they stand. What ships is the round: re-gloss every zone from the store, re-project the lattice, install, rebuild the chain books, the search index, the pointing recount, the pins, two receipts, the door and every book page, the lane state, the ledger index, the manifest. Fourteen tools; `build.sh` names five of them.

- The round lives in scratch scripts. The copy on the notes branch calls three scripts that were never kept.
- A failed stage does not stop it (`a && b` under `set -e` does not exit on `a`), so "FINISH-OK" can print over the previous search index or one book on the old store.
- It names twelve books by hand, the day's date in two places, and a scratch folder it always installs from, so a tool that rewrote a zone in between is undone (the readers found this; my own README warned about it for one tool only).
- `PIPELINE-MANIFEST.md`, written by the round's last step, says "build.sh calls 19 of the 120 tools" and lists the round's own tools under "not called by any build stage".
- Four hand-run steps belong in it and are not: the two that write the ledgers' divisions and the years onto the store's rows (the round's stated precondition), the per-book counts of the dictionaries beside the store (forgotten once: L9), and the count stamps.
- 29 of the 42 tools nothing calls are steps run by hand when the corpus lane sends something. The order of what follows what is written nowhere. The fourth reader wrote it out by trigger (`readers/tools-nothing-reaches.md`, part B2); it goes into the repository.

## 6. The page file

16,384 lines: a sheet of 2,380, a body, and one script of 13,816 lines that is a single closure. 659 statements share their state; 18 functions are longer than 120 lines; the one that builds the card (`openHud`) is 1,974, and inside it the three functions that must agree on what a block reads are keyed by the block's letters. The readers' findings cluster in exactly those places: what a line says is composed in five painters that have drifted; the pair's memory is keyed three ways; "does this run read whole" is decided twice.

- Unused: seven names, eight style classes, one custom property. Dead branches that wait on data no zone carries: about 700 lines (word-anchored commentary, part heads, omissions, three more), which is where L11 hid.
- Comments: 41% of the script's bytes and 49% of the sheet's. They are accurate about why and often wrong about what: the readers list about twenty that describe the behavior of two or three rulings ago, six of them within fifty lines of code that contradicts them.
- The script is not in strict mode, which is how an undeclared name shipped. A linter pass finds it in seconds and is now a check.
- Step 4 of the rebuild plan of 23 October (the engine in modules) is still the remedy for the shape, and is its own piece of work. The readers' nine "refactor first" places (three each) are the seams it should start from.
