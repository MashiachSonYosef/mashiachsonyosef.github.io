The owner, 15 November: «explain to me the justification for removing Peru and any time we wrote our own rule to remove?» Then: «we need to think in terms of each dictionary as a category», and «removing stuff shouldnt be seen as any more taboo than adding something that shouldnt have been added».

Four readers read the store, the card, the queued relays with the owner's own words, and the Peru card; four checkers tried to refute each reading. The whole inventory, 152 items with the checkers' verdicts: `INVENTORY-TABLE.md`. The readers' notes: `LENS-*.md`; the checkers': `VERIFY-*.md`.

## Peru

At Genesis 1:22 word 5 («be fruitful») the store holds 23 rows; seven say Peru, from four dictionaries. One was withheld: English Wiktionary's «Peru (a country in South America)». Wiktionary's own data files that entry as a name; TAHOT and MACULA do not call the word a name; the owner ruled the names gate on 13 November («1-3. ok agree», shown «Bara» as the example). The other Peru rows (OmegaWiki, PanLex, ConceptNet) stand: those dictionaries were never read for a name mark.

The checkers found something worse on the same card. Wiktionary's own right readings, «to bear fruit, be fruitful» and «to procreate, multiply, increase», are withheld as «pointed otherwise», because the store carried one linked form of Wiktionary's entry (the past, pa-ru) instead of the entry's own table, which lists the place's imperative (pe-ru). Meanwhile Wiktionary's «Pro-» and «his bull» stand, because the store carried them with no pointing at all. Both are our carriage, not anything Wiktionary says. Shelf-wide: 30,750 row-and-place pairs withheld that way (693 keys), and 514,078 standing unpointed, of which 193,547 Wiktionary points otherwise than the place.

## Where the rule was ours

**Whole dictionaries.**
- 14,146 rows of five tables (PanLex M24 and M82; Wiktionary's translation tables M78, M99, M103) struck on 31 August as «declares itself non-Hebrew». Those words were our catalog's label, read by our pattern; the dictionaries name a language for every row and the strike never read it. The rows include Biblical Hebrew (89 stem-tagged rows in M82: «(to) be holy (Qal)») and Aramaic («synagogue»).
- 79,095 rows struck on 1 September because our label named no language («silence is not a declaration»): Jastrow (back since 2 October, on the owner's word), and still out: Davidson 1855 (12,492 rows), ETCBC (4,246) and STEP's expanded Strong (1,431), three Bible dictionaries by their own titles.
- Harkavy (376 rows): struck because our cache folder's name held «yiddish»; no row was checked.

**The store's own build.** One row per key and words, credited to one source: no one ruled it. With the whole-source strikes it lost 4,256 readings of dictionaries that were never struck (v188 lands them in the next ship).

**Row lists.** Strong's records under bare letters (173) and two suffixes (34), struck as mis-filed with the mechanism never shown; 23 halves of our own cuts (two whole renderings are still missing from 15 cards); 3 unabridged BDB rows; 6 grammar words («the owner's ask», no words found).
