## Done, by the owner's list

1. **The English reader's order** (asks 7, R1-R6). A maqaf no longer turns the
   English around: a chain's or a run's English reads left to right in
   reading order (`to | all | Israel`, `and | to | every | where` with the
   middle maqaf's word in the middle, `to | the | woman`), while the chain's
   Hebrew stays as the scribes wrote it, right to left with each maqaf
   between the words it joins, so the ties cross as they do inside one word.
   A preview, `?chain=ltr`, lays the chain's Hebrew words left to right too,
   each under its English; it puts every maqaf one slot from where the text
   writes it (see `shots/english-reader-deut-1-1-chain-ltr-preview.png`
   against `shots/english-reader-deut-1-1.png`). The owner's call.
2. **The one hop** (R2). In the English reader a possessive suffix (my, your,
   his, her, its, our, their, as the pieces themselves read) stands before
   the piece it is written after: `for | your | house`, `at | my | post`,
   `on | their | nakedness`, `and | to | its | setting`, `and | to | all |
   its | neighbors`. Nothing else moves; the Hebrew reader keeps every piece
   under its own letters. OPEN: the owner said the uniform order "could be
   argued"; this is the English order the owner leaned to. "came + you"
   (עד־באכם) is not a possessive and does not hop.
