A space our own build made in Strong's and BDB closes where the text prints, as the corpus lane's lists name it

The store serves «Amos , an Israelite prophet» where the provider's file holds `<def>Amos</def>, an Israelite prophet`. Our build turned a field of the provider's XML into text by a step that put one space where each tag stood. The corpus lane traced every such space in the two Open Scriptures dictionaries (relay v168, 12 November): a space is made when the provider's file holds tags and no whitespace there, and a made space closes unless closing would join two things the provider's markup alone held apart. One list per source names, for each served text, the places that close: Strong's 4,847 texts and 6,418 closes, BDB 4,864 texts and 20,547 closes; 16 made spaces are kept.

The store's texts are not changed. Every list, ledger, lattice card, stem key and strike names a row by its served bytes, and the corpus lane's own words are that closing is a print step, run last, after the ledger's division. So the places ride on the row and the readers leave those characters out when they print.

- data/made-spaces-v1/ keeps the two lists, the lane's rule and its receipt byte for byte, under a pin that names each by the sha256 the relay names.
- tools/apply-made-spaces-v1.mjs writes row[9] = [the route text's closes or null, the definition text's or null] on a row of a listed source whose text is a line with a close, and on no other row: 23,230 rows (4,849 with a close on the route text, all Strong's; 23,230 on the definition text). It refuses, in words: bytes that are not the pin's, a malformed line, a row with a print slot and a close on its route (none today), a row of an unlisted source carrying the slot, a span-store row whose text has a close, and, while the store is the version the lists were read from, any count that is not the lane's. A second run moves nothing and writes nothing.
- With the slot taken off every row, 0 of the 256 shards differ from the shards before, as JSON text. The store's version moves from 90d9eb8e7082 to a8429861023d; the shards weigh 129,923 bytes more.
