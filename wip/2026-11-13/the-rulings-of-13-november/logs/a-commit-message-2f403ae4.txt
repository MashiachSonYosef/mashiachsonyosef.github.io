The unabridged BDB's made spaces close at print, and the UBS dictionary's "literally ...; hence ..." pairs are one reading again

Two changes to the store's slots, on the corpus lane's relays of 13 November and the owner's ruling of the same day. No text of the store is touched by either: of 652,954 rows set side by side with the live store's, 649,530 are the same in every slot, 137 gain the closes slot, and 3,287 change the slot that says where they divide.

1. The unabridged BDB (relay v178). The lane traced the spaces our own build put where a tag stood in the unabridged BDB's file, as it did for the two Open Scriptures dictionaries in relay v168: 137 served texts hold 186 made spaces, and every one closes. Its list is kept beside the first relay's, byte for byte, and tools/apply-made-spaces-v1.mjs writes the places on the 137 rows that serve those texts. A row prints «staff, rod, shaft.» where it printed «staff, rod, shaft .».

The lists now come relay by relay. data/made-spaces/ holds one folder per relay as the lane keeps it (made-spaces-v1 of v168, made-spaces-v2 of v178), with one pin over both, and each list is read with the receipt of its own relay: which source it is, and the store version it was read from. A source has one list. The second relay's receipt also says of two sources that they hold no made space (the Open Scriptures lexical index in the first, the Wikisource Strong's in the second), and the index now names them as read.

The same relay names 750 texts of the unabridged BDB where a space must never close: our build took whole Hebrew words out of the opening of a sense, each leaving one space, as in «often named with , and then». That space stands for words of the provider's. The tool refuses a list that would close one, and the check holds that none does (85 texts stand in both files; no close falls on such a space).

When are a source's rows the rows its list was read from? The tool refuses a count that disagrees with the lane's only while they are. Until now that meant "the store is the version the list was read from", which stopped being true the moment a second rule moved the store. It is now also true when every move since is this rule's own, or when the source's rows hold exactly the keys and texts they held when an earlier run found them so (the index records a sha256 of them, per source). So the lane's counts stay held across a ledger answered anew, as below, and stop being held, and are only said, once a row of that source is struck or landed.

2. The UBS dictionary's branch B01 (the owner, 13 November: «1-5. agree go ahead», call 3; relay v176). The UBS dictionary often gives the Hebrew word for word and then what that means: «literally: to dig (an area); hence: = action by which humans carefully examine an area ...». On 6 November the owner answered that these divide, and an outside reader then read the two halves as breakage. The answer is now "whole", with what it replaces written beside it in data/ledger-branch-answers-v1.json. tools/apply-ledger-divisions-v1.mjs took 3,288 dividing marks off 3,287 rows (one row took two steps): 3,286 rows that were two readings are one, and one row still divides at its "alternatively".

What moved, measured against the live build. The round was run whole (40 works, 453,713 words). Of the 400,242 lines under the words, 3 read otherwise, all in the Tikkunei Zohar (59a, 70a and 140a, one word each: «literally: what is torn apart» is the whole pair now), and none moved by a closed space. The search index holds 3,288 fewer route pills (748,847 to 745,559) over the same 646,344 rows. The twelve chain books hold 4,240 fewer links at rest (435,246 to 431,006) and 5,898 fewer with every switch lifted (734,372 to 728,474), and 229,531 distinct readings where they held 232,600; their words, their cards and every other count are the same.

Held by tools/check-made-spaces-v1.mjs, rewritten for the relays (M1 to M10): each relay's files are the bytes their pin names; every line is well formed and no list closes a space that stands for words taken out; every row of the store carries exactly its line's places; the index's record of each source's rows is a recount's; the lane's own counts are the store's for all three listed sources (the unabridged BDB: 137 rows close, 52 still print a space before a comma, the lane's 52); the page's functions and the door's give what the store reader gives; and 16 words were pressed in a browser, 1,717 pills read. The check was then made to fail on purpose in eight ways, each file put back byte for byte: seven turn it red (a row without its slot, a close moved one place, the page keeping a space, a list naming another place, an unabridged BDB row losing its slot, a list closing a never-closed space, the index's sha256 altered). The eighth does not: where the index says a source's rows are no longer the rows its list was read from while they still are, the lane's counts are said and not judged, which is what an honest store moved on by another rule looks like too. That is left as it is here and written down; the next change to this tool records the fingerprint the rows had when read, so the check can tell the two apart.
